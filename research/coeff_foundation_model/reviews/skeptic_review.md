# Skeptic expert review (verbatim agent output)

Files reviewed: `mechanism_tests.md` (primary target), `cross_level_operator_analysis.md`, `blocks_and_assumptions.md`, `DESIGN_HANDOFF.md` §§3–13, `artifacts/optical_tree_stats.json`. All quoted numbers were spot-checked against the JSON; the arithmetic in the docs is accurate (survival, lift, Markov residual 2.97×–20.9×, ~983 survivors/chunk, fan-out 43/206/398, p99/p1≈1354× at D10 all reproduce). The problems are not in the numbers — they are in what the numbers are taken to mean and in how the decision rules are written.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. [HIGH] Every pre-registered decision rule hinges on an ε that is never defined — no metric primacy, no units, no variance budget, no tuning protocol

T-A4: "adopt the smallest capacity within ε of the W7 ceiling." T-B3: "adopt the cheapest topology within ε of C4." Tier-1 (cross_level §8): "V-cycle within few-% of column-attention L2." As written these rules are not executable:

- **ε is in unspecified units on an unspecified scalar.** T-B3 lists three metrics ("per-band recon, worst-band recon, L2") with no aggregation or primacy rule. These will rank variants differently by construction: signal-space L2 is dominated by coarse bands (p99 ≈ 14k at D10 vs 46 at D1), so fine-band attenuation — the exact failure mode the topology shoot-out exists to detect — is nearly invisible in L2. Meanwhile "worst-band recon" will be dominated by D1 (9 coeffs/chunk, sig:noise the weakest, a band the plan is considering *dropping*). A topology decision driven by the least-important band, or blind to the bands that matter, are both possible under the rule as written.
- **No uncertainty model.** Nothing in the battery specifies seeds, event-level error bars, or what "within ε" means relative to run-to-run noise. Tiny AEs (the §12 instrument) have seed-to-seed spread that can easily exceed plausible W2-vs-W6 gaps. With an 8-rung W ladder × 5-entry C menu run "as one experiment grid," you are making ~dozens of comparisons against a noisy ceiling with no multiplicity control — the smallest-capacity-within-ε rule will systematically select undertrained/lucky small variants (winner's-curse toward W0–W2).
- **"Parameter-matched where possible" breaks the ceiling semantics.** If W7 (fully per-level) is parameter-matched to W0, each level gets ~1/L of the width and W7 is no longer a capacity ceiling — it's a different structure at the same budget. If it is not matched, "within ε of ceiling" conflates structure with raw parameter count. Same for C4 at matched params (see finding 5).
- **No per-variant optimization budget/LR protocol.** Optimal LR differs systematically across these variants (hypernetworks W4 and MoE W5 are notoriously LR- and init-sensitive; per-level W7 has 1/L the gradient signal per weight). One shared LR will produce ranking artifacts larger than the effects being measured.

**Fix:** pre-register, per test: (a) one primary scalar (e.g., per-band asinh-coefficient MSE, averaged over a *named* band set with named weights, with signal-space L2 and per-band tables as secondary/diagnostic); (b) ε := k·σ_ceiling where σ_ceiling is the std of the ceiling variant over ≥3 seeds × held-out event folds, with k stated; (c) a small LR sweep (3 points) per variant with best-of selection, identical step budget, and a check that all variants' losses have plateaued; (d) define W7's ceiling role as "generously parameterized and tuned," and report parameter-matched W7 separately as a structure comparison, not a ceiling.

### 2. [HIGH] The tree-lift → "hard-code tree coupling" inference is confounded by a common-cause signal envelope — and the plan's own data shows it

DESIGN_HANDOFF §3.2 calls the 31×/29× lift "THE decisive measurement" and §4.2 freezes "the wavelet tree is real structure worth hard-coding" as *not up for relitigation*. But activity lift P(child|parent)/P(child) cannot distinguish "parent→child tree-edge dependency" from "both coefficients sit inside the same broadband pulse." Two pieces of the team's *own* optical data point at the envelope explanation:

- The **shift sweep is nearly flat**: P(D2 child | parent shifted ±3 cells) varies only 0.152–0.157; D5: 0.548–0.564. A parent three cells away (i.e., *not* the tree parent) is almost exactly as predictive as the true tree parent. A genuine edge-specific dependency would peak sharply at s=0; a shared envelope produces exactly this plateau.
- The **lateral A10*→D10 lift (1.9×) ≈ the D10→D9 parent lift (2.1×)** — a non-tree, same-level conditioner does about as well as a tree edge at coarse scales.
- Additionally, the pooled lift is inflated by **between-chunk heterogeneity** (Simpson-type): pooling over 16,157 signal chunks of wildly varying activity makes the marginal P(child) low and the conditional high partly because parent-activity flags "busy chunk." The architecture always operates *within* a chunk with full context, so the design-relevant quantity is the within-chunk (or activity-stratified) lift, which will be substantially smaller — exactly as the team already saw when smart removal dropped TPC lift from 48× to 31× by removing one shared common cause.

This doesn't mean tree coupling is wrong — it means the lift table doesn't license it over alternatives (within-band dilated convs + any coarse envelope conditioning), and certainly doesn't justify "not up for relitigation." T-B1 (within-band-neighbors-first predictability curves) is precisely the right control and is in the plan — but the conclusion is already settled in the handoff before T-B1 runs.

**Fix:** (a) recompute lift stratified per chunk or conditioned on chunk total activity; (b) add a lateral-control column to T-B1's curve: gain from tree ancestor at Δ vs gain from a *same-band* neighbor at the equivalent physical distance, and from a shifted non-parent at the same level — tree edges earn hard-coding only if they beat these controls; (c) demote handoff §4.2 from "settled" to "gated on T-B1."

### 3. [HIGH] The non-Markov residual is misread — and its *actual* architectural implication (support holes break the relay) is missed

mechanism_tests intro: "the measured non-Markov structure says distant levels carry unique information." §9.2: "the node state must carry it — supports d_s not-too-small."

- **The inference as stated is unsound.** Thresholding destroys Markovianity generically: if underlying *values* are exactly tree-Markov (the HMT's hidden chain), the binary activity of |c|>t is non-Markov — the doc even notes this ("exactly what the hidden Markov tree model predicts") and then still uses the residual lift to motivate distant-edge topologies (C1/C2) and d_s capacity. A 3–21× activity residual is fully consistent with a one-hop topology over continuous states and says nothing about whether *learned-state* relaying needs help. Also: P(child | parent inactive, grandparent active) conditions on a parent that is *sub-threshold, not absent* — the censored parent value is the obvious mediator.
- **The real implication goes unstated.** The encoder computes on active sites only ("edges exist only where the receiving endpoint is active"; "missing parent → learned null embedding"). So when the parent is inactive, the C0 down-sweep *cannot* relay grandparent→child at all — the relay node doesn't exist; the child gets a null embedding. From the JSON: P(parent active | child active) = cond·P(parent)/P(child) ≈ 0.73 at D6 — i.e., **~27% of active D6 coefficients are orphans whose ancestral context is structurally cut in C0.** The Markov-residual table is direct evidence that these orphans are common and informative. This is a topology argument the doc could legitimately make — but it's about *support holes*, not about d_s, and it changes which variants help (C2's ancestor-chain attention skips holes; widening d_s does nothing for a node that isn't there).

**Fix:** (a) compute and report the orphan fraction per band (active child, inactive parent, active ancestor at Δ≥2); (b) add to T-B3 a stratified read-out: recon error on orphaned vs parented coefficients per topology — this is the single sharpest discriminator between C0 and C2 and currently no test stratifies on it; (c) decide whether inactive *interior* tree nodes on active cones should be instantiated (cheap: cone occupancy is high) — a variant absent from the C menu.

### 4. [HIGH] AE reconstruction is the master decision metric, but the deliverable is a representation — and ground truth that could anchor decisions semantically is available and unused

Every adopt/reject rule in T-A4, T-B3, and Tier-1 is AE-recon-based. Two problems:

- **Without a specified bottleneck, recon can't discriminate.** "Per-chunk AE at matched params" never states the bottleneck. With generous width and no aggressive bottleneck, *every* topology (including the rounds-0 control) can pass coefficients through nearly unchanged — the comparison saturates and "within ε of C4" becomes vacuously true for C0. Topology only matters under the production constraint (anchor-level tokens × d). The doc's own honesty point (cross_level §4: "what's on trial is the up-sweep summarization + pooling jointly, as a function of (N,d)") is not reflected in the T-B3 spec.
- **Recon ≠ downstream usefulness**, and the plan has truth labels sitting on disk: doraemon provides per-interaction `tpc_de/pdg/positions` and `pe_counts` (mechanism_tests §4). A 10-line linear probe (predict PE count / deposited energy from frozen features) is a cheap, semantically grounded tiebreaker that appears nowhere in the battery. A variant pair indistinguishable on recon can differ a lot on whether cross-scale features are *organized* for prediction. T-B6 (telegraph probe) partially covers this but has no decision rule attached — it "reads on" things without gating anything.

**Fix:** (a) pin the AE bottleneck to the production token geometry (anchor level, d from the §12 ladder) and state it in T-A4/T-B3; (b) add a pre-registered secondary criterion to T-B3/T-A4: frozen-feature linear probe on pe_counts (optical) / tpc_de (TPC), with the rule "a topology may not be adopted if it is within ε on recon but loses >δ on the probe"; (c) give T-B6 a decision rule (it's currently the only test immune to "the AE didn't need long range" and it gates nothing).

### 5. [MED-HIGH] C4 "full attention = oracle" assumes connectivity = achievable performance — the same fallacy the document rejects one section earlier

mechanism_tests §2 (correctly): "connectivity in 2 sweeps is graph-theory, not fidelity." Then the C menu labels C4 full attention "(oracle, toy scale only)" and T-B3 adopts "the cheapest topology within ε of C4." Full attention is a connectivity superset, not a performance ceiling: at matched parameters and tiny data, attention over ~1k coefficients can *underperform* structured operators (worse inductive bias, harder optimization, known small-scale attention pathologies). If C4 lands *below* C2 on recon, the rule "within ε of C4" is satisfied by everything and selects C0 spuriously. Conversely, the oracle is only run at toy scale (~1k coeffs/chunk) while the decision is applied at production depth/width — topology gaps (oversquashing especially) grow with depth and with the token bottleneck, so a toy-scale tie does not transfer.

**Fix:** define the ceiling as max over {C4, C2, C3} (best-of envelope), not C4 alone; pre-register a scale-revisit trigger (re-run the top-2 topologies at ViT-S scale before trunk commitment); report C4-vs-structured ordering itself as a diagnostic — if C4 < C2, the "oracle" framing was wrong and ε-of-C4 rules are void.

### 6. [MED-HIGH] "Optical is dense within chunks ⇒ dense conv" is a category error: 62.5% is *cell-union* occupancy; coefficient-level occupancy is ~2.7%

cross_level §9.5 / blocks_and_assumptions header: "Settled since the handoff: optical uses dense within-level convs (measured 62.5% coarse-cell occupancy)." The 62.5% is the fraction of 1024-tick coarse cells where *any* of ~511 descendant slots is active. At the coefficient level — what a dense per-band conv actually iterates over — occupancy is ~983 survivors per ~36k-coefficient chunk ≈ **2.7%**, and per band it ranges from 36% (D10) down to 1.1% (D2) and 0.05% (D1). Dense conv may still *win* (these bands are small; gathers are launch-bound; the JSON's own per-band sizes suggest dense is plausibly faster) — but the decision was "settled" on a metric that doesn't measure what the decision needs, and the right evidence (T-B7 microbench dense-vs-gather per band) is queued but positioned as a cost test, not as the gate for this settled choice.

**Fix:** un-settle the dense/sparse choice in blocks_and_assumptions; gate it explicitly on T-B7 with per-band dense vs gather timings; if dense is kept for fine bands, note that the FLOPs are ~99% zeros there and confirm that the zero sites are masked out of the loss/normalization (active-site statistics differ from dense-tensor statistics by 30–2000×).

### 7. [MED] "Naive τ>>1 alignment near-optimal" is concluded from a metric that is insensitive to misalignment by construction

§9.3 retires open #18 from the activity shift sweep. But P(child active | parent shifted) is flat across *all* shifts (D2: 0.1516–0.1569 over ±3; D7: 0.647–0.662) — the metric barely responds to a 3-cell deliberate misalignment, so its failure to find misalignment is weak evidence. Activity is envelope-driven (finding 2): a pulse spanning many cells makes the conditional flat regardless of group delay. Misalignment damage is **value-level** — energy splitting between neighbors, sign structure — which matters directly for the ordered 2-child concat in the up-sweep (child_even/child_odd identity shifts under delay error). Also D9/D8 are "still rising at the sweep edge" (s ≤ −3), i.e., the sweep didn't bracket the optimum at exactly the levels where coif3 group-delay accumulation is largest.

**Fix:** (a) compute the coif3 per-level group delay analytically (it's a known filter property, not something to estimate from data) and bake δ_ℓ into the index map; (b) if measuring anyway, use a value-level statistic: R² of |child| regressed on |parent| (or mutual information of asinh-values) vs shift, and extend the sweep until the coarse-level curves turn over; (c) keep the activity sweep only as a sanity check. TPC's shift sweep has not been run at all (open #18 "mostly retired *for optical*") and TPC's field response makes delay structure worse — run it before the TPC index maps are frozen.

### 8. [MED] T-A5's decision rule validates physical-scale conditioning by beating a straw man

"physical-scale conditioning must beat level-index on transfer to justify the unified-axis story." Level-index is a degenerate competitor across modalities — TPC level-1 (1 µs) and optical level-1 (2 ns) are three decades apart in physical scale, so index conditioning transferring badly is foreordained, and beating it confirms nothing about the continuum story. Meanwhile the "tiling" claim itself (§1: modalities "tile an almost continuous physical-scale axis... meeting near ~1 µs") overstates: the supports barely *abut* — optical spans 2 ns–1 µs, TPC 1–8 µs; only one octave overlaps. A W4 hypernetwork trained on optical must **extrapolate**, not interpolate, to cover TPC's range; nothing in T-A5 tests extrapolation specifically. And scale is not the only covariate shift: noise structure (white vs coherent), polarity (negative-going vs bipolar/unipolar), and response shape all change together across modalities, so frozen-transfer deltas can't be attributed to the conditioning variable.

**Fix:** (a) replace the level-index baseline with the real alternative: a freely learned per-(modality, level) embedding; (b) add a *within-modality* held-out-level test (train W4 on optical levels {1–8,10}, evaluate level 9) — this isolates scale-interpolation cleanly; (c) add a held-out-end test (drop levels 9–10, evaluate) for extrapolation, which is what TPC transfer actually requires; (d) keep the 5 µs/10 µs SER pair (genuinely good control — best-designed item in T-A5).

### 9. [MED] T-A2 (CKA on tiny per-band models) and T-A3 (gradient-cosine) carry decision weight their known failure modes can't support

- **T-A2:** CKA between 1–2-layer per-band denoisers is dominated by the highest-variance feature directions; all bands' tiny denoisers will converge toward scaled matched filters of the SER kernel → high CKA *regardless* of whether one shared filter set serves all bands. Conversely, seed variance in tiny models can produce low CKA between functionally identical solutions. "Aligned cosine" of filters needs a stated alignment procedure (permutation/sign matching) — unspecified. And the decision target is wrong: similarity of *separately trained* models is neither necessary nor sufficient for "one shared model suffices" — which T-A4 measures directly anyway. No threshold for "high similarity" is registered.
- **T-A3:** per-band gradient cosines are noisy, layer- and training-phase-dependent, and Adam preconditioning changes effective conflict; the multi-task literature after PCGrad shows raw gradient conflict predicts multi-task outcomes poorly. Worse, per-band losses here have wildly different scales (active counts differ ~20× across bands: D4 215/chunk vs D1 9/chunk) — without per-band loss normalization, the cosine structure mostly reflects count imbalance.

**Fix:** demote both to *screening* (choosing which W-ladder rungs to run, as the priority list already implies) and strip their "Decision:" clauses; if kept, pre-register: CKA variant (linear, debiased), seed count, alignment procedure, the per-band loss normalization for T-A3, and the training window over which "persistent" conflict is evaluated. The binding decision should rest only on T-A4's direct loss comparison.

### 10. [MED] T-B1's predictability curves: censored values, class imbalance, and clustering will distort the curve that "parameterizes everything"

The priority list makes T-B1 the keystone ("they parameterize everything"). Three estimator problems:

- **Censoring:** ancestor *values* exist only when the ancestor is active; for inactive ancestors the predictor sees a missingness indicator — which reintroduces the thresholded-binary confound (finding 3) exactly where the test is supposed to escape it ("whether activity non-Markovness survives conditioning on values"). The incremental gain of Δ=2 given Δ=1 will be inflated wherever the Δ=1 parent is censored.
- **Imbalance:** activity prediction at D1 has P = 5.2e-4. AUC at 1:2000 imbalance is near-meaningless (a tiny score shift produces large AUC moves; AUC is insensitive to the precision regime that matters).
- **Clustering/uncertainty:** all statistics pool coefficients within chunks within 100 events; coefficients in a chunk are strongly dependent. A coefficient-level train/test split leaks chunk identity and inflates predictability; effective n for generalization is ~100 events, and no event-level CI is specified for the incremental-gain curve — yet edge-reach decisions (C1 vs C2) will be read off where that curve "vanishes."

**Fix:** split by event (not chunk, not coefficient); report average precision (or calibrated lift at fixed recall) instead of AUC for fine bands; bootstrap CIs over events on the incremental-gain curve and pre-register the "gain has vanished" criterion (e.g., upper CI < x% of total explainable gain); for censoring, run the value-regression arm restricted to the subpopulation with active ancestors at all Δ considered, alongside the full-population arm — the difference is itself the censoring effect, worth reporting.

### 11. [MED] The factorial T-A4 × T-B3 is run jointly but decided marginally — interactions will produce an incoherent adoption

Priority item 4 says "run as one experiment grid, not two," but the decision rules are stated per-axis ("smallest W within ε of W7"; "cheapest C within ε of C4"). Interaction is likely *a priori*: shared edge weights (W0–W2) make the scale axis a depth-10 RNN — precisely the attenuation regime where C2/C3 help — while per-level weights (W6/W7) can learn per-hop gain compensation, making C0 look adequate. Marginal rules evaluated at default settings of the other axis can adopt (W2, C0) when only (W2, C2) and (W6, C0) are actually within ε.

**Fix:** pre-register the joint rule: adopt the cheapest (W, C) *cell* within ε of the grid's best cell, with cost = T-B7-measured ms/event of the combination; report the interaction table, not two margins.

### 12. [MED] "Flat attention suffices for optical" is settled using a token count that the anchor sweep (T-C1) is allowed to change

blocks_and_assumptions Block 5: optical trunk = flat global attention at ~5.8k tokens — but Block 4 says the anchor level is "a measured knob, not a constant," with level 9/8 giving ~11.5k/~23k tokens. At 23k tokens the handoff's own measurements (13.3 ms fwd / 52.6 ms fwd+bwd at ~30k) put flat attention firmly in the "must use hierarchy" regime. The trunk decision is sequenced before the tokenizer decision it depends on, and no test re-opens it.

**Fix:** condition the trunk statement on the anchor outcome ("flat iff anchor stays at level 10"), and add the per-anchor trunk cost row to T-C1's output so the anchor decision internalizes its trunk consequence.

### 13. [MED] The "noise chunk" class (|x|max ≤ 50 ADC ≈ 19σ) is contaminated, and the sig:noise columns built on it are quoted as physics

Signal chunks are |x|max>50 ADC with σ=2.57 ADC — so "noise" chunks admit real light up to ~19σ. A quick consistency check: at κ=1.2, the universal threshold is ~5.3σ, so pure-Gaussian D1 survival should be ~1e-7; the measured noise-chunk D1 survival is 4.2e-5 — **~300× the Gaussian expectation**, i.e., the "noise" chunks demonstrably contain structure (sub-50-ADC signal and/or non-Gaussianity). This matters because conclusions are drawn from these columns: "D1 is NOT a TPC-style constant noise floor (sig:noise 41×, signal-bearing)" — both numerator and denominator are contaminated, and these classes also seed any future activity-prediction labels. The doc nowhere propagates this; the campaign's signal-chunk definition is inherited silently.

**Fix:** either validate the noise class against truth (doraemon is noise-free — chunks with zero true PE are *exact* noise after the forward model) or report the Gaussian-expected survival next to measured per band so contamination is visible; re-state the D1 conclusion accordingly.

### 14. [MED] The measured tree statistics come from the noisy 10 µs dataset, but training will run on noise-free doraemon + a forward noise model — and no test gates the swap

mechanism_tests §4 notes tree statistics "should be re-measured" on doraemon once a loader + forward optical noise model exist — but no decision rule exists for *disagreement*. Every architecture decision in T-B/T-C is parameterized by light_output statistics (lift curves, occupancy 62.5%, fan-out, survivals) measured with real noise and the 10 µs SER; doraemon has a 5 µs SER (halving the response footprint in band units — shifting which bands are "mid" vs "fine" in the W6 grouping) and synthetic noise. If the doraemon-measured occupancy or lift differs materially, the dense-conv choice, the anchor level, and the regime grouping all silently inherit stale premises.

**Fix:** make the doraemon re-measurement a Tier-0 gate with a pre-registered equivalence band (e.g., per-band survival within ±25% relative, occupancy within ±10 pts); pin which dataset's statistics are authoritative for each downstream decision.

### 15. [LOW-MED] T-B4 gradient-reach norms: no input distribution, no norm, no threshold — sensitivity ≠ information

‖∂(band-j out)/∂(band-k in)‖ is input-dependent (which events? active sites only?), norm-dependent (Frobenius? spectral? estimated how?), and measures sensitivity, not transmitted task-relevant information — a trained net can have small Jacobians along directions the task doesn't use (then small reach is fine) or large Jacobians that carry nothing. The doc correctly says it must be combined with T-B1, but registers no joint criterion, so T-B4 currently can't change any decision — it's decoration with a measurement cost.

**Fix:** either attach the joint rule ("if T-B1 shows needed gain at Δ and T-B4 shows reach decay > x per hop at Δ for topology C, C is disqualified") or drop T-B4 and let T-B6 (the telegraph probe, which measures functional transmission directly) carry this role — it's the better instrument and is already in the battery.

### 16. [LOW-MED] Missing tests for decisions the plan will definitely face

- **A-band handling.** A10/A4 is unipolar, different units, 100% kept, and gets a bespoke "root partner" edge type (cross_level §3) — no test in the battery exercises it. The lateral A10*→D10 lift (1.9×) is the only measurement, and it used *thresholded* A10 (A10 is unthresholded in production — the starred conditioning doesn't match the model's input). Add an A-band ablation arm to T-A4 (A as root-partner vs A as just-another-level vs A excluded).
- **Quantization.** Production quantizes survivors to 12 bits; all measurement scripts and (presumably) all probe training use unquantized values ([ASSUMED, minor] in Block 0). Fine-band survivors have p50 ≈ 20 — 12-bit quantization noise relative to that is not obviously negligible for value-regression targets. One column in T-A4 (train/eval on quantized) closes it for the cost of a cast.
- **Forward-noise realism for optical** (related to finding 14): no listed test compares forward-noise doraemon statistics to real-noise light_output at matched SER — confounded by the SER difference, so it needs the 10 µs forward-model config or an explicit acknowledgment that this is unmeasurable with current data.
- **Sequence-packing/cross-event contamination** is dismissed as "engineering, not a design risk" (cross_level §7) — but packed events sharing attention windows is a *correctness* risk for the trunk, and nothing tests it. One masked-vs-padded equivalence check suffices.

### 17. [LOW] Cross-modality strength comparison and regime table are presented with more precision than the estimators support

"optical D2 (P=0.011, lift 14×) vs TPC D3 (P=0.014, lift 31×) — same structure, roughly half the strength" (§9.6): lift is mechanically inverse in the marginal and both numbers are pooled over different chunk/event mixtures with different preprocessing (smart removal vs per-chunk σ) — "half the strength" is a composition-sensitive statement. Similarly the W6 regime grouping {coarse, mid, fine, A} is derived from *activity* statistics (T-A1 "DONE") but groups weights for *value*-processing functions; it's fine as a candidate (and the doc treats it as one), but the "[DONE — the measured tables]" framing slightly overstates what was measured. Low priority because the joint statistic mostly motivates rather than gates.

## Summary of the three structural themes

1. **Activity statistics are doing value-level work throughout.** Lift, non-Markov residual, shift sweep, occupancy — all are thresholded-binary statistics, and the threshold itself induces dependence and censoring. Findings 2, 3, 7, 13 are all instances. The single highest-leverage correction is to add value-level twins (regression R² / MI on asinh values, with event-level CIs) to every tree statistic before locking topology decisions, which T-B1 partially does — make it fully do so.
2. **The decision rules are pre-registered in name but not in operational content** (findings 1, 5, 9, 11, 15): no ε, no primary metric, no seed/CI budget, no tuning protocol, no joint rule for the factorial, ceilings that may not be ceilings. As written, most rules could adopt the wrong variant while technically being followed.
3. **The strongest available evidence is underused:** doraemon truth labels (probes as decision tiebreakers, exact noise-chunk definitions), analytic group delay (instead of an insensitive sweep), and the orphan-fraction statistic implicit in the existing JSON — all obtainable this week at T-B1 cost.
